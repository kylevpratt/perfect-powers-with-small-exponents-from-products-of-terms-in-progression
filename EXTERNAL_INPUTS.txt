External theorem interfaces for the perfect-powers formalization

The final entry point is PerfectPowers.mainResults_of_external_inputs in
PerfectPowers/Main/Conditional.lean. Its four explicit hypotheses are:

1. External.AffineFaltingsStatement
2. External.SuperellipticGenusStatement
3. External.RemondStatement
4. External.PrimeAPStatement

These are proposition definitions and theorem arguments, not globally
declared mathematical axioms. The final theorem is conditional on these four
permitted inputs; it does not claim to prove Faltings, the genus bound,
Remond, or the draft's uniform prime-AP estimate.

The project treats these four statements as standing external hypotheses.
Proofs of these inputs and effective computability of k_0 are outside the
project's scope.

Affine Faltings

This input is the standard affine-plane formulation over Q. An absolutely
irreducible nonsingular plane equation whose geometric genus is at least two
has finitely many rational zeros. Absolute irreducibility is irreducibility
after mapping coefficients to AlgebraicClosure Q. Nonsingularity is the
Jacobian criterion at every zero over that algebraic closure.

The passage from the usual smooth-projective Faltings theorem to this
coordinate formulation, including the standard geometric interpretations
of the Jacobian criterion and genus invariant, is inside the external
theorem boundary of this project. We do not claim that passage has
been proved in Lean.

Concrete geometric genus

For an AffinePlaneCurve, form the actual rational coordinate ring

    A = Q[X,Y] / (F),

and its fraction field L. Irreducibility is supplied as a proof, so A is an
integral domain. In Omega[L/Q], consider, for each discrete valuation
Q-subalgebra R of L having fraction field L, the Q-linear span of

    { a * d(b) : a,b in R }.

The intersection of these spaces is the space of rational differentials
regular at every place, including places at infinity. The definition of
geometricGenus is its Q-dimension. This is a concrete regular-differential
definition, not an arbitrary numerical function or a genus formula assigned
to our family. Both external statements use this identical definition.

For one-variable geometrically integral function fields in characteristic
zero, this is the usual geometric genus of the smooth projective model.
That standard identification is included in the accepted formulation of the
external inputs; its proof and a projective model are not constructed here.

Genus lower bound

The second input states that for ell >= 2, B a nonzero integer, and C a list
of distinct positive integer shifts of length branchCount ell, the equation

    B*y^ell = product_j (x+C_j)

has the above geometric genus at least two. branchCount ell is 6 for ell = 2
or 3, and ell otherwise. The hypothesis is uniform in B and C. Proving it is
outside the project scope. An exact genus formula is neither assumed nor
used as the definition of geometricGenus.

Internal geometry and finiteness deductions

Curves/SuperellipticGeometry.lean proves irreducibility after every
characteristic-zero field extension: a simple root provides an Eisenstein
prime. It also proves the Jacobian criterion from separability of the product
of distinct linear factors. Neither proof uses the genus input or Faltings.

Curves/AffineFiniteness.lean applies affine Faltings to this proved geometry
and the external genus lower bound. Its conclusion supplies the exact
rational-zero finiteness premise used in the main proof. The main theorem
also supplies Lemma 3.6 internally. No additional mathematical input for
affine finiteness or odd-progression nonsquareness is needed.

Other two inputs and project scope

External.RemondStatement retains the corrected bound
exp(5^(D^4) * log(M) * log(log(M))) for M >= 3, and requires finiteness before
the numerical bound is applied. External.PrimeAPStatement retains the
uniform error estimate of the paper draft's Lemma 3.3.

The threshold target is existence of an absolute k_0, uniform in every
allowed exponent. Effective computability of k_0 is not a project goal.
Computing effective prime-AP constants is not a required extension.
